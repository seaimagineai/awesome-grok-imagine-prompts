## 1. Teacup Ferry — a tiny ferry crossing a cup of tea

[TXT](text/en-US/09-miniature-and-surreal-1.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** text-to-video · **Output:** 10s · 16:9 · 720p

```text
A continuous macro shot of a pale ceramic teacup on a breakfast table, filmed at the waterline with a 90 mm macro lens. Inside the cup, a tiny yellow ferry with one enclosed cabin carries three stacked sugar cubes across still amber tea. The cup rim stays visible at the back, establishing the impossible scale; morning window light gives the liquid a transparent copper edge.

During seconds 0–3 the ferry emerges from behind a floating lemon slice, pushing a narrow V-shaped wake. At 3–7 seconds the camera tracks sideways at its speed as the ferry passes one stationary teaspoon resting against the far rim. During 7–10 seconds it slows beside a small cork landing, the wake gently rocks the cork, and the bow stops just short of touching it.

Sound: delicate water movement, a low toy-sized motor hum, one quiet ceramic tick from off-screen. No music or voices. Keep the ferry, cabin and three cubes unchanged. The lemon does not chase the boat; the tea never becomes an ocean. No crew appearing, spilling liquid, extra vessels, cuts, lettering or logos.
```

## 2. Desk Drawer Weather — a miniature rainstorm inside a desk drawer

[TXT](text/en-US/09-miniature-and-surreal-2.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** text-to-video · **Output:** 10s · 16:9 · 720p

```text
An overhead three-quarter view of a real oak writing desk in soft afternoon light. A partly open drawer contains a small rectangular model courtyard: slate paving, one copper drain and a single paper umbrella planted upright in a clay stand. A compact grey rain cloud hangs only inside the drawer. The desk surface and a plain closed notebook remain completely dry.

For seconds 0–3 hold the composition as fine rain strikes the tiny paving and beads collect on the umbrella. From 3–7 seconds slowly push closer: rivulets follow the grout into the drain, while the umbrella shakes once under a heavier drop. From 7–10 seconds the cloud thins, rain stops and one final drop falls from the umbrella edge. End on the draining courtyard, still visibly contained by the wooden drawer.

Sound: intimate tiny rain taps, soft trickling and the last isolated drip. No thunder, music or narration. Keep one cloud, one umbrella and one drain throughout. Preserve the drawer dimensions and paving pattern. Water obeys gravity and stays inside the model; no flooding desk, growing trees, hands, cuts, text or magic sparkles.
```

## 3. Terrarium Lift — a tiny elevator ascending inside a glass garden

[TXT](text/en-US/09-miniature-and-surreal-3.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** text-to-video · **Output:** 10s · 9:16 · 720p

```text
A vertical macro view through the clean curved glass of a terrarium. Inside, a single brass cage elevator rides on two straight rails fixed beside a moss-covered stone tower. A fictional adult gardener figurine in a cream coat stands inside, holding one small watering can. Ferns frame the lower corners; the real terrarium lid is visible overhead. Diffused overcast daylight, tactile handcrafted materials, restrained green and brass palette.

At 0–2 seconds the cage waits at the bottom and its narrow gate swings shut. At 2–7 seconds the elevator rises steadily; the camera tilts upward to follow without orbiting. At 7–10 seconds it stops level with a tiny fern balcony, the gate opens, and the gardener remains inside looking toward the plant. Finish before any step out.

Sound: a gentle mechanical whirr, a small gate click and muted indoor room tone. Keep the gardener adult, miniature and visually consistent; the can stays in one hand. Rails remain straight and attached, with plausible cage clearance. No extra floors, duplicated people, giant insects, glass warping, sudden scale changes, readable labels, music or cuts.
```

## 4. Pocket Moon Laundry — a paper moon drying above a rooftop

[TXT](text/en-US/09-miniature-and-surreal-4.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** text-to-video · **Output:** 5s · 9:16 · 720p

```text
A surreal handcrafted miniature rooftop at blue hour, viewed close enough to see the fibres of its cardboard parapet. A thin clothesline spans two wooden poles. Hanging from two ordinary clips is a small circular paper moon, its warm ivory glow lighting one terracotta pot below. A much larger dark city silhouette sits softly out of focus behind the miniature set; there is no second moon in the sky.

For the first second, hold still on the slightly bent paper disk. Over seconds 1–4 a mild breeze turns the moon gently on the line, revealing its paper-thin edge before it swings back toward the camera. Make a very slow forward camera move, keeping both clips in frame. In the final second it settles almost flat; the moving pool of warm light settles on the same pot.

Sound: paper flutter, a faint line creak, distant city air. No score or speech. Both clips remain attached and the disk keeps the same size and circular outline. Light follows the disk consistently. No orbiting planets, fabric morphing, falling clips, stars appearing, fast spin, hands, text, cuts or lens flare.
```

## 5. Bookmark Canyon — a folded paper bridge spanning an open book

[TXT](text/en-US/09-miniature-and-surreal-5.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** text-to-video · **Output:** 10s · 16:9 · 720p

```text
A close lateral view across a large open blank sketchbook on a dark wooden table. Its gently arched pages form two chalk-white canyon walls. A narrow red paper bookmark has been folded into a footbridge across the central gutter. One tiny fictional adult hiker in a blue jacket stands at the near end. Warm desk-lamp light throws crisp paper shadows; visible fibres and imperfect folds make this a practical tabletop world.

At 0–3 seconds the hiker tests the bridge with one boot; the fold flexes slightly under the weight. At 3–7 seconds track slowly left as the hiker takes three careful steps across, using outstretched arms for balance. At 7–10 seconds the hiker reaches the opposite page and sits on its edge, feet dangling above the gutter. The bridge relaxes behind them without collapsing.

Sound: tiny boot taps, dry paper creaks and quiet room air. No narration or music. Preserve one hiker, the blue jacket, continuous red bridge and blank pages. Keep scale and gravity consistent; no printed words, page turns, extra limbs, growing bridge, camera dive, abrupt cuts or real mountain landscape.
```

## 6. Cloud in a Colander — a kitchen utensil releasing a miniature cloud

[TXT](text/en-US/09-miniature-and-surreal-6.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** text-to-video · **Output:** 5s · 16:9 · 720p

```text
A restrained surreal kitchen still life: a stainless-steel colander sits on a pale stone counter under a broad window. A small dense white cloud rests inside it like soft steam with a defined rounded top. Keep a folded grey cloth beside the colander and the tiled backsplash fixed. Photograph it as a real object with cool daylight and accurate brushed-metal reflections, not a cartoon.

During seconds 0–2, several soft wisps slowly push down through the existing holes while the main cloud remains inside. During 2–4 seconds those wisps merge beneath the bowl into a single smaller cloud hovering just above the counter. A subtle camera slide to the right reveals the gap under the colander. In the final second one tiny rain drop falls from the lower cloud onto the stone and makes a visible dark dot.

Sound: quiet kitchen air and one distinct water tick. No boiling, sizzling, music or voice. The colander never changes shape or hole pattern; reflections move with the camera. Keep both cloud masses small and white. No hands, flooding, lightning, huge smoke plumes, disappearing cloth, text, logos or cuts.
```
