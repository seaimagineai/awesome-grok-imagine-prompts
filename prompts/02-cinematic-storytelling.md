## 1. Last Tram Note — restrained urban romance

[TXT](text/en-US/02-cinematic-storytelling-1.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** text-to-video · **Output:** 12s · 16:9 · 1080p

```text
Opening frame: the final tram of the night waits at a quiet riverside platform after rain. Two
fictional adults stand under separate warm lamps, six meters apart. One holds a folded paper
note; the other watches the tram doors. Grounded contemporary romance, natural faces, wet stone
reflections, cool river haze, 50mm lens feeling, no recognizable city or transit branding.

0–4s: slow lateral dolly. The note-holder gathers courage, crosses two measured steps, and the
paper edge trembles in the breeze. 4–9s: the other person turns at the soft door chime. Their
eyes meet; neither speaks. The note is offered, hand to hand, with natural contact and one tiny
relieved exhale. 9–12s: tram doors begin to close in the distant background, but both remain on
the platform. Camera eases closer and ends on their shared, uncertain smile.

Audio: river, light rain runoff, electrical tram hum, one door chime, paper movement, breathing.
No score, dialogue, narration, or subtitles.

Continuity: stable identities, coats, note, lamp positions, tram geometry, spatial distance,
and rain direction. No face drift, hand fusion, melodramatic embrace, sudden crowd, readable
ads, camera cut, or time jump.
```

## 2. Room 407 — quiet hotel mystery

[TXT](text/en-US/02-cinematic-storytelling-2.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** image-to-video · **Output:** 10s · 16:9 · 1080p

```text
Preserve the supplied old hotel corridor, room door, patterned carpet, brass wall lamps, trolley,
and lone fictional detective. Maintain the symmetrical framing, amber practical light, and deep
green shadows. The mood is tense and realistic, not supernatural horror.

0–3s: locked camera with a nearly invisible push-in. The detective notices a thin line of water
emerging from under the closed door. It follows the carpet's slope and darkens the fibers
physically. 3–7s: they crouch, touch the water with two fingertips, then look toward the silent
service trolley as one wheel rotates a few centimeters from the uneven floor. 7–10s: a telephone
rings once behind the door. The detective freezes and slowly raises their gaze to the room number.
End before the door opens.

Audio: distant ventilation, rain on a far window, fabric movement, one trolley-wheel squeak,
single muffled telephone ring. No music, whispers, jump scare, or dialogue.

Continuity: exact corridor layout, identity, wardrobe, door closed, trolley contents, water path,
and light level. No ghost, blood, weapon, moving walls, extra doors, flickering text, camera shake,
or new character.
```

## 3. Glasshouse Pursuit — grounded parkour action

[TXT](text/en-US/02-cinematic-storytelling-3.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** text-to-video · **Output:** 10s · 21:9 composition in 16:9 output · 1080p

```text
Opening frame: broad interior of an abandoned botanical glasshouse at blue hour, broken panes,
wet tiled paths, dense ferns, rusted irrigation pipes. A fictional courier in practical gray
clothes runs toward camera carrying one small weatherproof case. Cinematic realism, wide lens,
cool sky light and sparse warm maintenance lamps, no weapons or recognizable branding.

Create one continuous backward tracking shot. The courier runs four strides, plants one foot on
a low stone border, vaults over it with both hands briefly contacting the surface, lands with
weight and two recovery steps, then slides under a fallen irrigation pipe. Leaves react to body
contact, droplets shake from branches, shoes maintain friction, case stays secured against the
torso. Camera retreats on a smooth stabilized path with mild human vibration, never passing
through plants. End as the courier reaches a closed service gate and turns to listen.

Audio: breath, footfalls changing from tile to soil, fabric, case buckle, leaf contact, dripping
water, distant metal echo. No score, gunfire, crowd, or dialogue.

Avoid: impossible flips, weightless movement, limb distortion, duplicated case, broken glass
impact, teleporting camera, aggressive motion blur, or environment redesign.
```

## 4. Tidekeeper — coastal fantasy ritual

[TXT](text/en-US/02-cinematic-storytelling-4.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** image-to-video · **Output:** 11s · 2:3 · 1080p

```text
Preserve the supplied original tidekeeper character, weathered blue robe, shell staff, cliff
altar, tide pools, distant sea stacks, dawn cloud layers, and painterly-realistic style.

0–4s: the tidekeeper plants the staff into a shallow pool with visible weight. Concentric ripples
travel outward and reflect the true environment. Wind pulls the robe and loose hair consistently
toward the inland cliff. Camera cranes slowly upward from waist height. 4–8s: water rises from
three tide pools as narrow translucent ribbons, rotating around the staff without touching the
character. Small shells and stones remain on the ground. The character closes their eyes and
takes one controlled breath. 8–11s: the ribbons fall naturally as rain into the pools, the sun
breaks between clouds, and the character opens their eyes. End stable.

Audio: coastal wind, low surf, wood-on-stone staff contact, water lifting and falling, one deep
nonverbal breath. No spoken spell, choir, or explosive hit.

Continuity: exact face, robe layers, staff design, coastline, water volume, and wind direction.
No crown, extra symbols, glowing eyes, body levitation, ocean wall, lightning, text, or logo.
```

## 5. Paper Moon Delivery — hand-drawn animation

[TXT](text/en-US/02-cinematic-storytelling-5.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** text-to-video · **Output:** 9s · 4:3 · 1080p

```text
Opening frame: original hand-drawn 2D animation on textured off-white paper. A small night courier
rides a bicycle across a curved rooftop bridge, carrying a round lantern parcel. Simplified ink
outlines, limited navy, cream, vermilion, and pale yellow palette, visible pencil registration,
twelve-frames-per-second feeling, no resemblance to an existing studio or character.

The bicycle pedals at a steady handmade cadence while the bridge curves downward. The lantern
parcel sways with delayed weight; coat tails and two paper clouds move in different rhythm. Camera
pans sideways as a large paper moon rises from behind stacked rooftops. At second six, a gust
lifts three loose delivery receipts from the basket; the courier catches one against the handlebar
without stopping. Finish as the bicycle passes under a small arch and the moon fills the opening.

Audio: soft bicycle chain, paper flutter, tiny bell, distant night insects, minimal plucked-string
motif with four notes. No voice or text.

Continuity: stable line thickness, palette, bicycle structure, parcel, character proportions,
paper texture, and side-on perspective. No smooth glossy 3D, morphing wheels, extra limbs,
studio imitation, readable writing, or frame border changes.
```

## 6. Europa Signal — hard-science discovery

[TXT](text/en-US/02-cinematic-storytelling-6.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** extension from a previous clip · **Output:** continue 10s · preserve input ratio and resolution

```text
Continue directly from the final frame. Preserve the fictional astronaut's identity, pressure
suit, helmet reflections, tether, tool pack, ice tunnel geometry, headlamp direction, floating
ice dust, and the blue-white exposure of the source clip. Do not restage or cut away.

The astronaut holds the current pose for half a second, then slowly turns toward the pulsing
signal beneath the translucent ice wall. Their boots remain magnetically connected to the rail.
They raise the existing scanner with both hands; its narrow beam crosses the ice and reveals a
repeating geometric shadow moving far below, indistinct and physically refracted rather than a
clear creature. Camera continues the source clip's slow forward drift by less than one meter.
At second seven, the signal stops. The astronaut lowers the scanner slightly and says, quietly,
“Control, the pattern just answered.” End on their helmet reflection and a stable wall.

Audio: preserve the existing suit ventilation and tunnel resonance; add scanner chirps, glove
movement, and radio-compressed dialogue. No music or external explosion sound.

Continuity: no helmet removal, gravity change, new tools, visible alien, ice collapse, suit text
mutation, camera cut, subtitle, or sudden brightness shift.
```

## Story continuity tips

- Treat each 6–12 second generation as one shot, not an entire screenplay.
- Write a stable final pose into every shot if you plan to extend it.
- Carry forward the exact direction of gaze, wind, travel, camera movement, and light.
- Repeat only the visual facts that must survive; excessive restatement can compete with action.
- Let audio bridge cuts: a bell, breath, engine, or room tone can preserve continuity better than more visual detail.
