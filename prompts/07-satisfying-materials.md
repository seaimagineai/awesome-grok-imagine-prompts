## 1. Glass Fig — translucent fruit slicing

[TXT](text/en-US/07-satisfying-materials-1.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** text-to-video · **Output:** 10s · 9:16 · 720p

```text
Opening frame: an original sculptural fig made entirely from pale violet glass rests on a charcoal cutting board. Its polished skin encloses tiny amber glass seeds. Extreme side-on macro, the fig fills the lower two-thirds of the vertical frame, a plain dark background and a narrow warm rim light. A steel blade is already touching the top of the fig; only a cropped adult hand holding its handle is visible.

0–2s: the blade presses slowly, making a fine bright contact line. 2–7s: one deliberately surreal, clean cut passes through the solid glass as though through firm jelly; the blade never jumps. 7–10s: a single thin slice tilts away and settles on the board, exposing the seed cross-section. Camera stays locked; shift focus gently from the blade edge to the newly exposed surface.

Audio: a close crystalline scrape followed by one light glass tick as the slice lands. Quiet room, no music or voice. Keep all seeds fixed inside their slices and the remaining fig intact. No shards, exploding fruit, second cut, extra fingers, edible flesh, text or logos.
```

## 2. Velvet Fault — kinetic sand compression

[TXT](text/en-US/07-satisfying-materials-2.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** text-to-video · **Output:** 5s · 16:9 · 720p

```text
A rectangular block of fine cobalt-blue kinetic sand sits alone on a pale stone worktop. Three thin ivory layers cross the block horizontally. Shoot at tabletop height with a close 70mm macro feeling, raking light from the left revealing every grain. An unmarked square metal press hangs immediately above the block; its support extends beyond the top edge of frame.

0–1s: the press lowers until it just touches the flat sand surface. 1–4s: it compresses the block to half its original height in one steady motion. The center holds together while both sides split into soft stepped ridges, bending the ivory layers into continuous curves. 4–5s: the press stops; a few grains trickle down the front and become still. Camera makes a tiny lateral slide to reveal the ridges without changing height.

Audio: dry granular crunch, a faint metal mechanism hum, then silence. Preserve the blue and ivory layers, connected sand mass and realistic gravity. No liquid behavior, springing back, flying particles, hands, color change, dramatic music or lettering.
```

## 3. Pearl Track — a marble through ceramic channels

[TXT](text/en-US/07-satisfying-materials-3.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** text-to-video · **Output:** 10s · 16:9 · 720p

```text
Opening frame: one cream-colored marble waits at the high end of a handmade celadon ceramic track. The track has three broad descending curves and ends in a shallow circular bowl, all visible together on a walnut table. A low three-quarter overhead view establishes an uninterrupted route. Soft window light produces a small moving highlight on the marble; the rest of the studio is quiet and uncluttered.

0–2s: a wooden release gate slides sideways and the marble starts rolling. 2–7s: the camera tracks gently alongside as the marble follows all three curves, gathering speed on each slope and slowing against the outer bank. 7–10s: it enters the bowl, circles once and settles at the center. Keep the bowl in frame throughout the final action.

Audio: rolling ceramic rumble, three small seam clicks synchronized to visible joints, a diminishing circular rattle at the finish. No music. Exactly one marble, continuous motion and fixed track geometry. No teleporting, jumps between curves, impossible uphill acceleration, extra balls, numbers, branding or edits.
```

## 4. Copper Petal — thin metal embossing

[TXT](text/en-US/07-satisfying-materials-4.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** text-to-video · **Output:** 5s · 9:16 · 720p

```text
Top-down close-up of a small square of annealed copper foil lying over a shallow leaf-shaped mold on black felt. The foil already has a shallow leaf outline and fine side veins, with an unfinished central vein and four corners clearly visible. Two adult fingertips secure only its upper edge; one rounded wooden burnishing tool enters from below. Warm diffuse light reveals copper grain without mirror glare. Fill the portrait frame with the foil and leave a narrow felt border.

0–1s: the tool presses the center of the hidden mold. 1–4s: it travels once from the base toward the leaf tip, pushing the thin foil into the recessed central vein; the existing side veins stay unchanged while the central groove deepens. 4–5s: the tool lifts straight away, leaving the embossed leaf recessed into the still-square sheet. Camera remains perfectly overhead and stationary.

Audio: close soft metallic creak and the dry whisper of wood against foil; stop both when the tool lifts. Preserve the same fingertips, foil corners and mold location. The metal deforms rather than growing or melting. No automatic repeated stamping, torn foil, new tools, jewelry, labels or music.
```

## 5. Rain Skin — peeling a water-beaded silicone sheet

[TXT](text/en-US/07-satisfying-materials-5.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** text-to-video · **Output:** 10s · 9:16 · 720p

```text
A palm-sized translucent silicone sheet covers a smooth teal tile. Twelve clear water beads sit on the sheet, catching a large rectangular window reflection. Macro view at a low diagonal angle, cool daylight and a cream tabletop. One corner of the silicone is already lifted by a small pair of blunt tweezers entering from upper right; the tile itself is dry and immobile.

0–2s: the tweezers draw the corner slowly upward. 2–7s: a visible peel line crosses the tile from right to left; the flexible sheet curls into one broad arc while several droplets slide down its curved surface and merge near the lowest edge. 7–10s: one larger drop releases onto the tabletop and the peeled sheet hangs above the fully exposed tile. Follow the peel line with a slow macro push, ending with both sheet and tile identifiable.

Audio: restrained rubbery peel, delicate water movement and one soft drop. Keep silicone transparent, thickness constant and tweezers attached to the same corner. No foam, tearing, hand morphing, multiply appearing drops, tile bending, voice or text.
```

## 6. Ink Nest — marbling paper lifted from water

[TXT](text/en-US/07-satisfying-materials-6.txt) · [↑ Index](#index)

**Mode:** text-to-video · **Output:** 10s · 16:9 · 720p

```text
A shallow rectangular marbling tray holds cream water with loose concentric rings of indigo and rust pigment floating on its surface. One blank cotton-paper sheet lies flat on the pattern, its near edge already pinched by two adult hands at opposite corners. Overhead workshop view, soft north-window light, a linen cloth beneath the tray; no tools or writing elsewhere in frame.

0–2s: the near edge rises evenly, revealing a narrow band of transferred marbling. 2–8s: both hands lift together so the sheet peels progressively off the water from front to back. The camera tilts from overhead to a modest front angle, following the revealed pattern without losing the tray. 8–10s: the fully lifted paper hangs above the tray; two water drops fall from its lower edge and the image holds.

Audio: damp paper separation, a light tray ripple and two distinct drops. Transfer the same indigo and rust rings onto the paper, with natural watery edges. Keep the sheet rectangular and hands at their original corners. No new illustration appearing, dry instant transformation, folds, extra hands, captions or music.
```
